# Kindred 2.4.0

2.3.0 let one server host families for other people. 2.4.0 makes that something you can run: Kindred speaks eleven
languages, offers passwords and single sign-on beside passkeys, gives members and operators what data protection asks for,
and lets an operator open sign-up, decide feature by feature what each family may use, watch how the server grows and keep
it up to date. Try it on two invented families first:
[kindred-demo.niklas-heer.workers.dev](https://kindred-demo.niklas-heer.workers.dev).

## For everyone

- **Eleven languages.** Spanish, French, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Brazilian Portuguese, Japanese and Simplified Chinese join
  English, German and Afrikaans: every page, the words for relatives ("your great-aunt"), plurals, dates, heraldry,
  occupations and the questions of the week. Each language was translated and then reviewed by a second model.
- **More ways to sign in,** where your server offers them: a username and password with two-step codes (scan the QR code)
  and recovery codes, or single sign-on with Google, Microsoft, Authentik, Keycloak or another OpenID Connect provider. One
  account can have several ways in.
- **Your data, your choice.** Download everything your account holds, or delete your account, from the account menu.
- **New in Kindred** shows what each release brought, in your language.

## For stewards

- **Answer a relative's data request.** A copy, a restriction, or erasure of someone everywhere in the family (history,
  pictures, the feed and the assistant's conversations included), with the deadline in view.
- Features your operator manages show as locked, with one sentence saying why and how to reach them.

## For operators

- **Hosting mode and sign-up**: a landing page, and stewards may start a family themselves (Turnstile, terms, a processing
  agreement, 16 or older), within limits per day, network, account and in total, or with sign-up codes; a readiness
  checklist says what is missing first.
- **Policies**: the Campaign, the assistant, painted portraits, address suggestions and push, each off, left to families,
  or for chosen families only. AI can run on your key with per-family and server-wide monthly budgets, reserved before every
  call. **Announcements** reach stewards or everyone.
- **Sign-in tab**: turn passkeys, passwords and single sign-on on or off, add providers, and make a reset link; it refuses to
  lock the last operator out.
- **Statistics, Health and Updates**: growth and activity as counts (small numbers hidden), the daily jobs and backups, a
  release check after you agree to it, and a one-click Update for copies made with the Deploy button.
- **Privacy tab**: the record of processing (Art. 30), the subprocessors (also on `/legal`), a breach register with its
  72-hour clock, a retention job that reports before it deletes, and statements of reasons when you suspend or close a
  family. See [docs/gdpr.md](../gdpr.md) and the [processing agreement template](../processing-agreement.md).

## Upgrading

Back up first. This release adds twelve migrations to `migrations/accounts` (0038, 0040 to 0050), all adding tables or
columns. On Node they are applied when the server starts, after a copy of the accounts database; on Cloudflare,
`mise run deploy` applies them before the new Worker goes live. To go back to 2.3.0, restore that copy and start the old
version.

Things to know:

- **Nothing changes for a single-family install** until you change it: passkeys stay the only way in, the mode stays
  "single", every policy stays "each family decides", and the release check stays off until an operator agrees to it.
- **`KINDRED_SECRET` now also protects passwords and recovery codes.** Changing it invalidates every password; keep it.
- **Copies made with the Deploy button** set `KINDRED_UPDATE_VIA=github-actions` and can update from the console. Others see
  their own update steps.
- **From this release on, migrations are additive**: the previous release's code keeps working against them.

The full list is in the [changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md).
